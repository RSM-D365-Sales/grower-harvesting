Before you walk up

  • Open the Dashboard. Harvest Ready should read 4 and Pending D365 Sync 3, and the Upcoming Cuts should be dated today and tomorrow. If the dates are old, the seed has gone stale: paste supabase/migrations/008_bluestem_seed_data.sql into the Supabase SQL editor and run it. It is re-runnable and re-anchors every date to today.
  • Browser at 100% zoom, window at least 1280 px wide so the sidebar stays open.
  • Know the cast below. Every grower, crew member, customer and figure is Bluestem's fictional demo set. Never describe it as a live tenant or as executed ERP postings.
Don't press Test Connection, Extract from D365, Pull Orders, Pull Inventory, or Retry. They call the real D365 proxy, which has no credentials on the show floor, and will show an error. The inbound pages already hold the cached data you need. Start Harvest, Mark Complete and the phase-advance arrows are fine to click; they write to the demo database, so re-run the seed afterwards to reset.

The story in thirty seconds

Bluestem Greens is Bluestem Fresh Produce's greenhouse division: living lettuce and herbs in NFT and DWC bays, microgreens on vertical racks, high-wire mini cucumbers, and a propagation house feeding them all. Ingrid runs ten bays and rooms from this app. Today the butter lettuce in GH-1 Bay B is ready for the Great Lakes Grocers morning truck, pea shoots are mid-cut in Micro Room A, and yesterday's cucumber pick is stuck in the D365 queue on a batch error. Everything the crew records lands in Dynamics 365 as inventory journals and Report as Finished; everything D365 knows about items, production orders, demand and on-hand flows back.

Questions you will get

Is this actually talking to D365?

Yes, by design: inbound pages cache D365 OData v4 entities (ReleasedProductsV2, ProductionOrderHeaders, SalesOrderHeadersV2 and LinesV2, InventOnHandEntities) through a Supabase Edge Function that holds the Azure AD client credentials; outbound posts inventory counting journals and the Report as Finished action. The booth build points at the BFP-UAT environment with the extract buttons unwired, so what you see is the cached demo set.

We grow open-field, not greenhouse.

Same application. Bays and square feet are labels over the same fields-and-acres model; an open-field version of the seed exists. Crew scheduling, graded yields and the D365 queue don't change.

How does grading reach finance?

Grade rides on the journal line. A is premium retail, B foodservice, C processing to the Grand Rapids fresh-cut plant, reject is compost. The dashboard donut is the same split.

What about lot traceability?

Every packed item is batch-tracked in D365 (tracking dimension group Batch). The failed sync is the batch check doing its job: D365 refused a journal line for a batch it hadn't registered.

Why a sync queue instead of posting straight away?

So the greenhouse never stops when the ERP is busy. Records queue locally, post in order, and failures are visible with the exact D365 error and a retry instead of silently dropping.

Can the crew use it in the greenhouse?

The layout is responsive; the sidebar collapses on a tablet. The cut-by-cut yield form is three fields.

If something goes wrong

  • Dates or names look wrong, or a page is empty. Re-run migration 008 in the Supabase SQL editor; it clears and reloads every demo table in about two seconds. Then refresh the browser.
  • Someone pressed Test Connection or Extract and got a red box. "The UAT connection isn't wired on the show floor." Press Refresh on the page and carry on; the cached data is intact.
  • You advanced phases or completed cuts during a walkthrough. That's fine for the conversation. Re-run the seed before the next one so the story resets to "butter lettuce ready today".
  • Nothing loads at all. Check the booth network; the app reads from Supabase over HTTPS. Fall back to the looping video while it reconnects.
